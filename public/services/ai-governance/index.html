AI Governance & Safe Use

Practical AI governance without unnecessary complexity

Your employees may already be using ChatGPT, Claude, Microsoft Copilot or other AI tools at work.

The question is whether they know which tools are approved, what information they can enter and who is responsible when something goes wrong.

Nissanka AI Advisory helps small businesses put clear and proportionate AI guardrails in place. The aim is not to stop useful experimentation. It is to help people use AI with greater confidence while protecting sensitive information and reducing avoidable risk.

Discuss your AI governance needs

Is AI use happening without clear oversight?

Many small businesses have adopted AI informally.

An employee starts using ChatGPT to improve an email. A manager uploads a document for summarisation. A team begins testing an AI meeting tool. Before long, AI is being used across the business without a shared understanding of the risks.

You may be concerned that:

  • Employees are using personal AI accounts for business work.
  • Confidential information could be entered into an unapproved tool.
  • Different teams are following different rules.
  • Nobody clearly owns AI governance.
  • Your existing policies do not mention generative AI.
  • Employees are unsure when AI-generated work needs to be disclosed or checked.
  • Leaders do not know how to assess a new AI tool or use case.
  • Customers or business partners may ask how you govern AI.
  • Your organisation may not be prepared for emerging regulatory or contractual expectations.

A small business does not need an oversized governance framework. It does need clear decisions, practical ownership and guidance employees can understand.

What does practical AI governance involve?

AI governance is the way your organisation decides:

  • Which AI tools may be used.
  • Which accounts and licences are appropriate.
  • What information employees may enter.
  • Which uses require additional review.
  • Who is responsible for approving tools and use cases.
  • How AI-generated work should be checked.
  • What employees should do if information is entered accidentally.
  • How the organisation keeps its approach up to date.

Good governance should make responsible use easier. It should not create a long policy that sits unread in a shared folder.

What good AI governance looks like

After the engagement, your business should have greater clarity about:

  • Which AI tools may be used.
  • What employees should never enter.
  • Who approves new tools and use cases.
  • Which uses need additional review.
  • How AI-generated work should be checked.
  • What happens when an incident occurs.
  • How governance supports rather than blocks useful adoption.

The objective is proportionate control.

A low-risk drafting task should not require the same process as an AI system influencing a decision about a customer or employee.

Frequently asked questions

Does a small business really need an AI policy?

When employees use AI for work, some form of clear guidance is advisable. The policy does not need to be lengthy, but employees should understand the approved tools, data boundaries and their responsibilities.

What is an AI compliance audit?

It is a practical review of how your organisation currently uses AI, where risks or governance gaps exist and which actions should be prioritised.

Will governance prevent employees from using AI?

It should do the opposite. Clear guidance can give employees greater confidence because they understand what is permitted and where they need approval.

Can you provide legal or regulatory certification?

Nissanka AI Advisory provides practical governance and readiness support, not formal legal certification. Where specialist legal, cyber-security or data-protection advice is required, this should be obtained from an appropriately qualified professional.

Can you review a specific AI tool?

Yes. A review can consider how the tool will be used, the information involved, available controls and the questions that should be raised with the supplier.
