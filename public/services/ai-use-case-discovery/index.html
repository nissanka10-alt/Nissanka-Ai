AI Use-Case Discovery & Prioritisation

Find the AI opportunities worth pursuing

Small businesses are surrounded by AI ideas.

Every week brings another tool, feature or promise of automation. The difficult part is deciding which opportunities are genuinely useful for your business and which are likely to consume time without creating meaningful value.

Nissanka AI Advisory helps small businesses identify practical AI use cases, compare their value and risk, and build a clear roadmap for what to test first.

Explore AI opportunities in your business

Do you have ideas but no clear starting point?

Many businesses know they should be exploring AI, but the conversation quickly becomes unfocused.

One team wants a chatbot. Another wants to automate reporting. Someone has seen an impressive demonstration online. Leaders may feel pressure to act, but there is no shared method for deciding what is worth the investment.

You may be experiencing some of the following:

  • Employees have many ideas but no way to prioritise them.
  • AI discussions start with tools rather than business problems.
  • The organisation has run experiments without measurable results.
  • Leaders are unsure which tasks are realistic candidates for AI.
  • Promising ideas are blocked by poor data or unclear ownership.
  • The potential risks have not been considered.
  • There is no clear pathway from an idea to a controlled pilot.
  • The business wants to act but does not want to waste money.

A useful AI roadmap should begin with the work that needs improving.

Start with the problem, not the technology

The strongest AI use cases are usually connected to work that is repetitive, slow, difficult to scale or unnecessarily dependent on manual effort.

Examples may include:

  • Producing regular reports or management commentary.
  • Summarising documents, meetings or customer feedback.
  • Drafting routine communications.
  • Finding information across internal documents.
  • Reviewing large volumes of written material.
  • Preparing first drafts of policies or proposals.
  • Improving customer-service responses.
  • Supporting employee onboarding.
  • Reducing administration in HR or recruitment.
  • Identifying patterns in operational information.

Not every frustrating task is a good AI use case. The opportunity also needs sufficient volume, appropriate data, a responsible owner and a manageable level of risk.

How use cases are prioritised

A practical use case should answer five questions.

1. What business problem are we solving?

The use case should address a genuine source of cost, delay, poor quality or employee frustration.

2. Is the work frequent enough to matter?

Saving ten minutes on something completed once a year will not create meaningful value.

3. Is the necessary information available?

AI cannot compensate for missing, inaccessible or unreliable information.

4. What happens if the AI is wrong?

The greater the potential impact, the stronger the review and control required.

5. Will people use the new approach?

A technically impressive solution creates little value when it does not fit the way employees actually work.

What happens after discovery?

Discovery should lead to a decision, not simply a long list of ideas.

Each use case should normally fall into one of four categories:

Start now

The opportunity is useful, feasible and low enough in risk to test through a controlled pilot.

Prepare first

The idea has value, but data, process, governance or employee readiness needs attention.

Explore later

The opportunity may become relevant but is not a current priority.

Do not pursue

The likely value is too low, the risk is too high or there is a simpler way to solve the problem.

This helps a small business focus its limited time and investment on the opportunities most likely to work.

Frequently asked questions

Do we need to know which AI tools we want to use?

No. The process begins with your business needs and workflows. Tool selection should follow the use case rather than come first.

How many use cases should we identify?

That depends on the size and complexity of the business. The goal is not to produce the longest list. It is to identify enough opportunities to make a sensible comparison and select a manageable starting point.

Can you focus on one team?

Yes. Discovery can focus on a single function such as HR, Finance, Operations, Sales or Customer Service.

Will every use case require new software?

No. Some opportunities may be addressed through tools the business already owns. Others may require a new service, integration or process change.

Can you help us run the pilot?

Yes. Support can continue through pilot design, employee preparation, governance, measurement and review.
