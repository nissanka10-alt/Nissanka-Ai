Practical AI Training & Adoption

Practical AI training that helps people work better

AI training should do more than explain what ChatGPT, Claude or Microsoft Copilot can do.

It should help people use these tools confidently in their real work, understand where they can save time and know when human judgement is still needed.

Nissanka AI Advisory provides practical AI training for small businesses that want to move beyond experimentation and help their teams use AI safely, consistently and productively.

Book an introductory conversation

Is your team unsure how to use AI at work?

Many small businesses already have employees experimenting with generative AI. The challenge is that usage is often inconsistent.

Some people are using it every day. Others are unsure where to start. Managers may not know what information is safe to enter, and leaders may struggle to see whether AI is creating any meaningful value.

You may be experiencing some of the following:

  • Employees are using personal AI accounts without clear guidance.
  • People have attended generic AI demonstrations but still do not know how to apply the tools to their roles.
  • AI-generated work varies in quality.
  • Teams are repeating the same manual work even though AI could help.
  • Leaders cannot see whether training is improving productivity.
  • A small number of confident users are moving ahead while the rest of the organisation is being left behind.

The answer is not simply more prompt training. People need examples that reflect the work they actually do.

Training built around real work

Every training engagement is shaped around your business, your employees and the tools available to them.

Rather than using generic examples, we look at the tasks your team completes regularly and explore where AI could help.

This may include:

  • Drafting emails, reports and presentations.
  • Summarising documents or meeting notes.
  • Improving the structure and clarity of written work.
  • Reviewing information and identifying themes.
  • Creating first drafts of policies, job descriptions or customer communications.
  • Preparing questions, plans and discussion guides.
  • Reducing repetitive administration.
  • Checking and improving AI-generated outputs.

Employees also learn where AI should not be used, what information should be protected and why AI-generated answers still need to be checked.

What the training can include

Leadership briefing

A practical introduction for owners and senior leaders covering opportunities, risks and what responsible AI adoption looks like in a small business.

Team workshops

Interactive sessions built around real workplace tasks. Participants learn by trying the tools rather than sitting through a technical presentation.

Role-based training

More focused sessions for teams such as HR, Finance, Operations, Sales or leadership.

Safe-use guidance

Clear explanations of what information should not be entered into public AI tools and how approved business accounts should be used.

Practical prompt and workflow examples

Reusable examples that employees can adapt after the training rather than starting from a blank page.

Follow-up adoption support

Support after the workshop to help teams apply what they learned, share useful examples and overcome early barriers.

Adoption and value measurement

Simple ways to understand whether employees are using the tools, where time is being recovered and which workflows are improving.

Ways to work together

One-off AI workshop

A focused session for a leadership team, department or small business.

This works well when you want to introduce the topic, build confidence or explore how AI could support a specific team.

AI capability assessment and training roadmap

A review of current confidence, usage, risks and opportunities, followed by a practical training and adoption plan.

Multi-week adoption programme

A more structured programme combining workshops, use-case development, safe-use guidance, coaching and measurement.

Ongoing AI advisory support

Regular support for leaders and teams as new tools, questions and opportunities emerge.

What should improve afterwards?

The aim is not to turn every employee into an AI expert.

It is to help your people:

  • Feel more confident using approved AI tools.
  • Identify tasks where AI can genuinely help.
  • Produce better and more consistent first drafts.
  • Spend less time on repetitive work.
  • Recognise poor-quality or unreliable AI output.
  • Understand what information must be protected.
  • Share useful practices across the business.
  • Use AI as part of everyday work rather than as a one-off experiment.

How the process works

1. Understand your business

We begin with a conversation about your people, current tools, common tasks and concerns.

2. Identify practical examples

We select realistic tasks and workflows that employees will recognise.

3. Deliver the training

Sessions are practical, accessible and designed for people with different levels of confidence.

4. Support adoption

Where needed, we provide follow-up support, guidance and measurement to help the learning turn into lasting behaviour.

Frequently asked questions

Do employees need previous AI experience?

No. The training can be adapted for complete beginners as well as people who already use AI regularly.

Can the training focus on one business function?

Yes. Sessions can be designed for areas such as HR, Finance, Operations, Sales or leadership.

Which tools can the training cover?

Training can cover tools such as ChatGPT, Claude and Microsoft Copilot, depending on what your business already uses or is considering.

Is this only a technical training course?

No. The focus is on everyday work, safe use, good judgement and practical business outcomes.

Can you help after the workshop?

Yes. Support can continue through a multi-week adoption programme or ongoing advisory arrangement.
